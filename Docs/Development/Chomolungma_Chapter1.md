# Chomolungma · The 12 chapters and Chapter 1 in detail

Version 1.0 · 2026-11-14 · Claude as designer, the author as director.

**Decided with the author today:** the game is called **Chomolungma** (the mountain's Tibetan name, "Goddess Mother of
the World"). The real 1:1 Everest stays exactly as built, seen through the eyes of the Sherpa people. The old Norse
layer is re-rooted in the mountain's own Tibetan Buddhist beliefs:

- the golden summit tree becomes the **Wish-Fulfilling Tree**
- the realm weather becomes the **Six Realms**
- the ghosts become souls in the **Bardo**

There are **12 chapters**. Voices are the author's job: every spoken line here is marked **[VO]**.

Labels used below: **[Have]** exists in the game today · **[New]** must be made · **[To measure]** a position or
number to read from the real terrain · **[To discuss]** the author decides.

## Part 1 · The twelve chapters in one sentence each

| # | Title | One sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Day One** | Sangye leaves Dingboche as the newest climbing Sherpa of a foreign expedition and ends the day at his father's memorial stone, where Nima lets slip that his father did not fall. |
| 2 | **The Puja** | Over Lobuche and Gorak Shep to Base Camp, where a lama blesses the climbers and their axes at the stone altar, and the first realm, the Hungry Ghosts, turns the camp's water into a fight. |
| 3 | **The Icefall** | Before dawn Sangye crosses the Khumbu Icefall for the first time on ladders and fixed ropes, carrying a load to Camp 1 while the ice groans under him. |
| 4 | **The Valley of Silence** | Up the Western Cwm to Camp 2, a furnace of sun by day and a freezer by night, and back down: the first rotation. |
| 5 | **Rest Days** | At Base Camp, clients quarrel, the radio crackles with other teams, and Sangye finds his father's last diary pages in the expedition's store tent. |
| 6 | **The Changed Ice** | On the second rotation a serac fall has rebuilt the Icefall, and the Cold Hells come down the mountain, blistering skin and cracking hands. |
| 7 | **The Lhotse Face** | On blue ice to Camp 3, a client falls on the ropes, and in the Bardo light Sangye sees his father sitting on the ridge far above. |
| 8 | **Retreat** | A storm drives everyone down to Base Camp, Nima finally tells half of the truth, and the forecast shows one summit window. |
| 9 | **The Push** | The last crossing of the Icefall and the Cwm, saying goodbye to the lower world one camp at a time. |
| 10 | **The Death Zone** | From Camp 3 over the Yellow Band to the South Col, where the Demigods' wind rages and a dying climber from another team asks for help. |
| 11 | **The Balcony** | On summit night Elena collapses at 8,400 m with Sangye's father 400 metres above, and the player must choose. |
| 12 | **The Forty-Ninth Day** | The Wish-Fulfilling Tree on the summit, or the long descent, and the end: smoke over Dingboche, or a new frozen figure facing the summit. |

Target length: about 70 minutes per chapter on average, 14 hours in all. Chapter 1 is about 65-75 minutes.

### 2.2 The research, turned into rules for this chapter

From the research done today (*Chapter1_Design.md* section 1, and the report *AI tools for cinematic chapters*):

| # | What the research says | Rule for Chapter 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The first minutes decide whether people keep playing (Google Play retention data) | Control within 20 seconds. The real game (thin air, a rope, wind, fear) is felt in the prologue, within 4 minutes |
| 2 | Tutorials barely help; help at the moment of need does (Andersen et al., CHI 2012, 45,000 players) | **No tutorial screens.** One short line, at the moment it is needed, spoken by a person (Nima, the radio) where possible |
| 3 | Teach without words (Mario World 1-1) | The trail teaches: a steep step teaches breathing, a narrow path teaches yak safety |
| 4 | One thing at a time (cognitive load) | One new mechanic per scene, never two |
| 5 | Four steps per idea: introduce, develop, twist, conclude (Nintendo, kishōtenketsu) | Every mechanic follows the ladder in 2.5 |
| 6 | Start in the middle, then show normal life (in medias res) | Prologue on summit night, then "Day 1" at dawn |
| 7 | Spaces tell stories (Carson; Jenkins) | The father's duffel, the photos in the tent and the memorial stone tell his story. Nobody explains it |
| 8 | A bond can be made by a mechanic (*Ico*: holding a hand) | Dolma holds your hand with a held button. The same input returns when Nima holds your rope, and at the very end |
| 9 | People remember the peak and the end (peak-end rule) | One designed peak (the memorial at dusk), one designed end (Nima's line in the dark) |
| 10 | Open loops pull (Zeigarnik effect) | Four questions stay open, and a visible count: **Day 1 / 49** |
| 11 | Autonomy, competence, relatedness keep people playing (self-determination theory) | Real choices (what to carry, Elena, the yaks, the promise), a first mastery (the rest-step), people who care (Dolma, the aunt, Nima) |
| 12 | Cinematic moments without cutscenes (Valve's *Half-Life: Alyx* commentary) | Look triggers with a timeout, sound before sight, light as a guide, people at a natural distance, exposition while walking |
| 13 | Dialogue while the player keeps control (*Firewatch*, *Oxenfree*) | Walk-and-talk with Nima; choices appear on screen while you keep walking; silence is also an answer |
| 14 | In climbing games the body carries the emotion (Cairn) | Breath, heartbeat, shaking hands, frost on the lens: the body is how the player feels the mountain |
| 15 | Pacing (our rule, 2026-11-02) | No stretch longer than 6 minutes without a decision, an event or a discovery |

### 2.3 Why a player wants to keep going after Chapter 1

1. **A mystery with a face.** The father speaks to you in the first minute, and you won't understand what he means
   until the last chapter.
2. **A secret held by someone you like.** Nima knows how your father died and won't say. That keeps you watching him.
3. **A ticking structure.** "Day 1 / 49" is always on the journal page. Every chapter moves the count, and players
   want to see where it ends.
4. **A glimpse of the myth.** At dusk the summit plume turns gold for a few seconds, and only Sangye sees it.
5. **Your choices come back.** The journal writes the first page from what *you* did: the promise, Elena, the yaks.
   The player sees that the story is theirs.
6. **Something no other game has:** the real Everest, walked on foot, through the eyes of the people who make every
   climb possible.

### 2.5 The teaching ladder (the introductory instructions)

One mechanic per scene, each in four steps. The **line** is the only instruction text; it is spoken by a person where
possible, and also appears once as a small caption.

| Mechanic | Introduce (safe) | Develop | Twist | Conclude | Who teaches · line | Min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Prologue: a taste, not a lesson* | walk along a rope in the wind | clip past one anchor | your oxygen mask frosts over; knock it clear | a slip into darkness | caption only: *Hold [Clip]* | 0-4 |
| Look and walk | the tent, still | step outside | the frozen ground crunches; your breath shows | walk to the duffel | none needed | 4-6 |
| Inspect | open the duffel | lift a photo, turn it | the photo has writing on the back | put it in your pocket | caption: *[Inspect] to look closer* | 6-12 |
| Hold a hand | Dolma takes your hand | she leads you to the camp edge | she stops and won't let go | she lets go when the aunt calls | Dolma [VO]: *"Hold tight, Sangye-dai."* | 12-16 |
| The pack | Nima drops the company load: 15 kg | add your own things, watch the weight | past 20 kg your walk slows | close it | Nima [VO]: *"Carry what you can carry tomorrow too."* | 17-21 |
| Oximeter and radio | Dr. Hale's radio check: clip it on | the number drops when you stand up fast | Elena's reads 82 and she laughs it off | it stays on your wrist from now on | Dr. Hale [VO] on the radio | 19-22 |
| Breathing and the rest-step | the first climb out of camp makes your breath loud | the hint comes when the breath is loudest | the slope steepens; sprinting empties you | at the top your breath calms | Nima [VO]: *"Slow is fast up here. Step, breathe, step."* | 22-27 |
| Water | your bottle is half empty | the stream by the trail | the edge is ice; kneel carefully | a full bottle | Nima [VO]: *"Drink before you are thirsty."* | 27-30 |
| The compass | Nima hands you your father's compass | it points north as you walk, and leads you through the cloud | at the memorial it trembles towards the hidden summit | it settles; you keep it | Nima [VO]: *"He never went anywhere without it."* | 21-53 |

**Not taught in Chapter 1:** ropes and ladders (Chapter 2, real ladder training at Base Camp), oxygen bottles
(Chapter 10), the realms (Chapter 2). The trek has no fixed ropes, as in reality.

### 2.6 The camera language

The player's eyes are the camera. The camera never leaves the body, except once.

**Rules**

1. **One cinematic only:** the prologue's fall turns into an out-of-body shot. The camera falls away from Sangye into
   the clouds, the night mountain is revealed, then the title. A short Sequencer shot; every other moment of the
   chapter is played.
2. **Gaze pull, never a grab.** A soft turn towards something important, at most a gentle nudge the player can resist.
   It ends as soon as the thing is in the centre third of the view. Used at most five times in the chapter.
3. **Look triggers with a timeout** (Valve's trick): an event waits until the player looks at it. If they don't within
   6-8 seconds, a sound pulls them (yak bells, a shout), and then it happens anyway.
4. **Sound before sight:** you hear the yak bells before you see the yaks, and the wind on the ridge before the plume.
5. **Light as a guide:** the white khata, the last sun on the memorial, Nima's headlamp in the dark.
6. **Inspect view:** a held object comes close, the background blurs, and you turn it with the mouse.
7. **Body awareness:** you see your hands (Dolma's hand, tying the khata), your breath fog, the sleeves of your father's
   red jacket.
8. **The body moves the view:** breathing sway that grows with altitude and effort, a small heartbeat push of the field
   of view (2-3°) at the peak only.
9. **Short takeovers only for the body:** the prologue slip (1.5 s tumble, then black) and kneeling at the memorial
   (0.8 s ease down; you can still look around). Every takeover starts and ends at the eye.
10. **No hard cuts in the present:** time jumps and sleep use a blink (the eyes close and open), never a cut.

**Every camera moment in the chapter**

| Min | Moment | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Black, wind, a heartbeat; eyes open on the rope | fade in from a blink |
| 2 | The father sits ahead in the snow | look trigger; a headlamp beam finds him |
| 3 | The snow breaks | 1.5 s tumble takeover, black |
| 3.5 | Out of the body, into the clouds, the night mountain, the title **CHOMOLUNGMA** | the only Sequencer shot |
| 4 | *Day 1.* Eyes open in the tent | blink |
| 8 | The photo of the father on the summit | inspect view, focus on the face, then turn it to read the back |
| 12 | Dolma runs up; you hear her before you see her | sound lure, gaze pull to the camp edge |
| 15 | The khata goes round your neck | look down: hands and scarf in view |
| 33 | Elena stops, hands on her knees | look trigger with a timeout |
| 40 | Yak bells up the trail | sound before sight; the yaks appear round the bend |
| 48 | The first prayer flags of the memorials on the ridge, backlit | light as a guide, slow gaze pull |
| 52 | Kneeling at the father's stone | 0.8 s kneel takeover, the khata in your hands |
| 53 | The summit plume turns gold above the ridge | look trigger; the heartbeat push of the view; the compass trembles |
| 60 | Nima's face in the headlamp light, then darkness | headlamp light only; his line; a blink to black |

### 2.7 The walkthrough, scene by scene

#### Prologue · The Balcony, 8,400 m, night (0-4 min)

Black. Wind. A heartbeat, too fast. Your eyes open into the beam of your headlamp: a frozen rope, a wall of snow, stars.
You walk. A clip is ahead, and the screen shows only *Hold [Clip]*. Your mask frosts over, the world goes white, and
you knock it clear. Ahead, a man sits in the snow without an oxygen mask, facing the summit. He turns his head. It is
your father.

**Father [VO]:** *"You came too early."*

The snow under you breaks. A tumble, black. Out of the body, the camera falls away into cloud, the moonlit mountain
rises out of it, and the title appears: **CHOMOLUNGMA**. Then, in small letters: *Day 1 / 49.*

#### Scene 1 · Dawn in Sangye's tent, Dingboche camp (4-12 min)

You wake in a two-person tent [New tent] with frost on its inner wall. Your breath shows. Outside: tents, blue barrels,
duffels, prayer flags snapping, a cook singing in the kitchen tent.

Your father's duffel lies at your feet. In it:
- his red down jacket, patched at the elbow (you are wearing its twin: he bought two)
- his mala (prayer beads)
- his summit certificate from 2019
- three photos:
  - your father and Nima on the summit, laughing
  - your mother, who died when you were small
  - you at Dolma's age, on his shoulders
- on the back of the summit photo, in his writing: *"Next time with Sangye. Not before he is ready."*

Nobody explains anything. The objects do. [The photos are renders of our own characters, never real people.]

*[To discuss: if we build one walk-in room, this scene could start in the family kitchen in Dingboche instead, with the
photos on the wall. The tent version needs no building.]*

#### Scene 2 · The farewell (12-17 min)

You hear her before you see her: Dolma, running up from the village with Aunt Pema behind her. Dolma takes your hand.
You hold the button to keep holding it, and she leads you to the edge of camp where the trail begins. The aunt ties a
khata round your neck.

**Aunt Pema [VO]:** *"Promise me you will come down."*

**Your choice** (shown while you stand with them):
- *"I promise."*
- *"I'll bring him home."*
- Say nothing.

Dolma pushes a folded drawing into your pocket: the mountain, a big figure and a small one. She lets go only when her
mother calls her. The journal will remember what you said.

#### Scene 3 · The job (17-22 min)

Nima finds you. He doesn't smile.

**Nima [VO]:** *"Your load. Fifteen kilos. Carry what you can carry tomorrow too."*

**Your choice: what to add to the company load** (the weight bar shows the cost):
- the thermos of butter tea (warmth at night)
- a second water bottle
- the first-aid kit with altitude medicine
- your father's mala
- Dolma's drawing (weighs nothing)

Past 20 kg, your walk slows.

Marek Kovač, the expedition leader, briefs the team: *"Lobuche tomorrow, Base Camp the day after. Nobody plays hero."*
Elena Ruiz, a first-timer, jokes about the cold. Ji-woo Han says nothing and films everything. Dr. Hale's voice comes
over the radio from Base Camp for the morning check: clip the oximeter on. You read 88. Elena reads 82 and laughs it off.

Nima hands you something wrapped in cloth: your father's brass compass.

**Nima [VO]:** *"He never went anywhere without it."*

#### Scene 4 · The trail (22-48 min)

The team walks up the valley, as on the real trek. Between the moments below there is only walking, breathing, the
river, the wind, and Nima's few words. *[To measure: the real memorials stand about 5 km up the trail from Dingboche,
roughly 35-40 minutes of pure walking at today's speed. The minutes below assume about 26; if our trail is longer, the
extra time goes into this scene (chapter up to about 75 minutes), and nothing is skipped.]*

- **22 · The first climb out of camp.** Your breath gets loud. Exactly then Nima says: *"Slow is fast up here. Step,
  breathe, step."* The rest-step appears.
- **27 · The stream.** Your bottle is half empty. You kneel on the ice edge and fill it. *"Drink before you are
  thirsty."*
- **33 · Elena stops,** hands on her knees: a headache, the first sign of altitude sickness. **Your choice:**
  - Carry part of her load. It costs stamina, and Nima nods.
  - Give her your water. It costs hydration.
  - Tell her to keep going. It's quicker, but she will be worse at Lobuche (Chapter 2).
- **34 · Walk-and-talk.** Nima asks: *"Why did you take this job?"* **Your choice**, shown while you walk:
  - *"For the money."*
  - *"For him."*
  - Silence.
  
  He answers each differently, and the trust between you begins here.
- **40 · Yak bells up the trail.** A yak train comes down a narrow stretch with a drop on one side. **Your choice:**
  - Step to the mountain side and wait. Safe.
  - Squeeze past on the outside. Faster, but a yak can shoulder you off the path onto a ledge: a fall, a scare, a hurt
    knee.
  
  Nima afterwards: *"Always the mountain side."* The player learns the rule by living it.
- **44 · Alone for a stretch.** Nima stays back with Elena. The cloud that rises up the valley every afternoon swallows
  the trail. Your compass points the way. Solitude is a tool here: for a few minutes it's only you, the wind and your
  father's compass.

#### Scene 5 · The memorials at dusk: the peak (48-56 min)

The cloud breaks. On the ridge ahead, backlit by the last sun, are dozens of stone chortens hung with prayer flags,
each for a climber who died on Everest [New props]. The wind is the only sound. Nima catches up and walks you to a new
stone, its edges still sharp. Your father's name is cut into it.

You kneel (a short body movement) and tie your khata to it. Above the ridge, the summit plume turns gold for a few
seconds. Your heartbeat rises, the compass needle trembles towards the hidden summit, and then it is gone. Nima saw
nothing.

#### Scene 6 · Night: the end (56-65 min)

A small camp below the ridge [New tents]: the team's tents, a stove roaring, tea. The evening radio call from Base Camp:
Dr. Hale checks everyone's numbers, and Elena's depends on what you did at minute 33.

You share a tent with Nima. In the dark, his headlamp on, he is quiet for a long time.

**Nima [VO]:** *"People say he fell. Your father did not fall."*

He switches off the light. Black. The journal opens and writes its first page by itself, from what you did today: the
promise, the load, Elena, the yaks, the stone. At the bottom of the page:

*Day 1 / 49.*
