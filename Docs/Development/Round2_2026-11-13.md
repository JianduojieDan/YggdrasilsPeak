# Round 2, 2026-11-13 — what changed and how to check it

Restore point: tag `restore-2026-11-13-before-round2`. Everything below is committed on local `main`.

## 1. Nothing floats any more
- One pass measures every placed object against the real ground (route detail tiles, else the landscape) and fixes it:
  `Tools/Placement/ground_pass.py` (~330,000 objects in ~30 s). 102,800 moved: small route stones, boulders over the
  glacier (up to 3 m in the air), ice blocks, camp props, oxygen bottles, pickets.
- Cause: the route rock generator read the ground height at the nearest metre (fixed: bilinear), plus terrain edits
  after things were placed. Run `ground_pass` (with APPLY) after any terrain or placement change.
- Note: right after a map loads, Nanite streams the ground in for a few seconds; screenshots then can show fake gaps.

## 2. Fixed ropes like the real route
- Yellow braided rope (dark tracer flecks) lying on the snow, pulled taut over bumps, sagging a little between anchors.
- Anchors every 10–25 m and at turns: aluminium snow stakes with a sling and screwgate carabiner; ice screws on the
  Khumbu Icefall and the Lhotse Face; rock pegs on the Yellow Band and the Geneva Spur.
- Faded old ropes of earlier seasons half buried beside about a third of the spans.
- `Tools/RoutePlanning/lay_fixed_ropes.py` (27 km: 1,092 stakes, 209 screws, 101 pegs).

## 3. Trees everywhere along the climb
- 38,000 trees (was 17,900), 16,000 of them above 4,400 m. Regions decide density (bare slopes / lone trees / groves /
  woods), height (×0.55–1.5) and the wind's lean; one species per grove: spruce low, burnt dead pines higher, golden
  ginkgo groves high up. Kept off the rope line, camps, icefall and steep slopes; seated on the lowest ground.
- Cost: see the numbers below; trees now stream in 6 km chunks, only near ones sway, distance follows View distance.

## 4. Planets and FPS
- Measured with the camera held on each planet (`Tools/Art/planet_perf.ps1`), at 1080p and native 1440p.
- A planet itself costs ~0.2 ms of GPU at native 1440p (shown vs hidden, same weather). Its realm's weather (particles,
  storm light) costs more. Shader made cheaper anyway (relief only where it shows, fewer noise octaves); looks the same.
- The bigger finding was the automatic quality bug (below), which kept the whole game on Low.

## 5. Settings that really work
- Root cause: the automatic quality saved every step down and never stepped up, so the game sat at Low quality and 50%
  resolution for good — the blurry snow, and settings that seemed to do nothing. Now your choices are the base and
  automatic steps last for the session only.
- Self-test `ygg.Settings.Test` steps every row through the menu: 26 of 32 change the engine at once (the rest were
  sliders already at 100% / frame generation tested without DLSS — re-tested).
- New: Upscaler (DLSS / TSR), Frame generation (Off / 2× / 3× / 4×, DLSS only), Low latency (Reflex), HUD style.
- NVIDIA DLSS 4.5 plugin 8.9 installed (Docs/Development/DLSS_Install.md); no login was needed.

## 6. Snow, footprints, the step snow
- Snow blur: mostly the Low/50% bug; plus a light sharpen after upscaling.
- Footprints: crisper walls up close, packed fine-grained floor, crumbly brighter lip, cold shade in the deep corners.
- Step snow: the big translucent "puff" sprites are gone; each step flicks 3–12 small solid snow clumps off the boot that
  land and melt in. Old puff to compare: console `ygg.Snow.Kick 2` (1 = new, 0 = none).

## 7. HUD v4 (menus unchanged, as asked)
- Watch: four thin rings (body, warmth, food, water) around the altitude and oxygen, bottom left; stamina appears as a
  short arc only while spent. Strip: one quiet line at the bottom centre. Settings > Game > HUD style.
- HUD: Everything (default, every reading) or Quiet (empty until your body needs you — then only that).
- No shadow pools: each letter and ring has its own thin dark edge.

## 8. Pack
- A flat lay, the way climbers photograph their gear: every item from straight above under one lamp, square to each
  other in three rows; a used-up item leaves a chalk outline on the tarp.
- Waiting for your OK: four realistic CC0 models from Poly Haven to replace the cartoonish compass, notebook, gas
  canister and stove (~25 MB).

## Numbers (standalone game, RTX 5070 Ti Laptop)
- Trees, eye level, High, native 1440p TSR: Dingboche 108 fps with the 38k trees / 102 without; 4,800 m 99 / 97 (noise:
  the trees are ~free). With wind sway on every near chunk it was 93 vs 119 seen from above - sway is now Epic-only.
- Planets, camera on the planet, native 1440p High: a planet ~0.2 ms GPU (shown vs hidden, same weather); all nine at the
  summit 112 vs 115 fps. Realm weather costs more than its planet; frames are CPU-limited (~10 ms) there.
- Settings self-test: 26 of 32 rows change the engine; quality change no longer resets the frame limit.
- Not verified by me (needs walking in the game): the new step snow clumps and the footprint shading - please look
  while you walk on snow (`ygg.Snow.Kick 2` shows the old puff to compare).
