1. One-page summary

What the chapter is

A young Sherpa, Sangye, leaves the expedition camp at Dingboche (4,410 m) on his first day as a climbing Sherpa for a foreign team. His father died high on Everest last season and his body is still up there. By night Sangye has walked the real trail to the Thukla memorials (about 4,830 m), tied a white scarf to his father's new memorial stone, seen the summit plume turn gold for a few seconds, and heard the older Sherpa Nima say in the dark: "Your father did not fall."

Everything is played in first person, on the real 1:1 terrain, built from a real elevation model. There is one cinematic shot in the whole chapter.

The feeling

Quiet awe, with grief underneath and a mystery on top. The art direction calls it "quiet awe": first "I could stand here", then "what am I standing in front of?". The body carries the emotion: breath, heartbeat, cold hands, a heavy pack. The myth enters only once, quietly, and only Sangye sees it.

The shape

Length

About 70-80 minutes. v1 said 65-75. The world builder now places the memorials 5.8 km up the trail (v1 assumed about 5 km). At today's walking speed (2.3 m/s) that is about 42 minutes of pure walking, plus the scenes. The robot player (section 7) will time a full run before the director plays.

How it serves the thesis

The thesis topic is a physiology model for high-altitude climbing, checked with a "virtual climber" (supervisor, 2026-10-26). Chapter 1 is where a player first feels that model: SpO₂ on the radio check, breath that grows loud on a climb, the rest-step, water, a pack that slows you, a client with altitude sickness. Every session also records its data through the flight recorder (section 7). The chapter is the story wrapper; the physiology is the core.

How strong the evidence is. Andersen et al. and the self-determination papers are peer-reviewed. The Google Play analysis is industry data. Nintendo, Ico, Alyx, Firewatch and flow are designers' practice from talks and interviews. The peak-end rule is well established in psychology but only applied to games by practitioners.

One honest gap. The camera spec keeps steady camera sway out of the 0.2-0.4 Hz band, where visually induced motion sickness is said to peak. The research note found no primary source for comfort limits of camera nudges or FOV changes on a flat screen (the Alyx comfort notes are about VR). So the camera limits are careful starting values, tested by measurement and by the director's own play, not proven numbers.

2.4 Why a player keeps going after Chapter 1

  1. A mystery with a face. The father speaks in the first minute: "You came too early."
  2. A secret held by someone you like. Nima knows how the father died.
  3. A ticking structure. "Day 1 / 49" on the journal page.
  4. A glimpse of the myth. The gold plume, seen only by Sangye.
  5. Your choices come back. The journal writes its first page from what you did.
  6. Something no other game has: the real Everest, on foot, through the eyes of the people who make every climb possible.

4. The camera language ("Eyes on a Body")

The full technical spec is Chapter1_Camera_Spec.md (2026-11-19). It scored three competing designs and combined the best parts. This section says it in plain words.

4.1 The idea

The camera is Sangye's eyes, sitting on a real body. The body explains every change of view the player did not make himself: a step, a breath, a landing, sitting down, a reflex to a loud sound, a small girl pulling his hand, a slip. If nothing in the body explains a move, the move does not happen.

4.2 Ten rules

  1. One writer. Only the new camera rig moves the view. The story asks the rig; it never turns the head itself.
  2. No spinning. Angles are always taken the short way round. Scripted shots are aimed at a point, never blended between two angles. (Blending angles the long way was the cause of the 360° vertical spin.)
  3. The body explains every move (see above).
  4. The eye is only low when the body is low. Lying, sitting, kneeling and crawling are real states. You cannot walk while you are low; moving stands you up first.
  5. Gentle speed limits for anything the story does: a nudge turns at most 20°/s sideways and 8°/s up or down.
  6. Everything the story holds, it gives back. Holding still, inspecting an object, the slip: each has an owner and a time limit, and is released automatically. A watchdog puts the camera back to normal walking if anything is left over. (In the code these are called "leases".)
  7. Cuts only with the eyes closed. Every jump in place or time happens behind closed eyelids or the title's black.
  8. The view is almost never locked. Only during the slip and the fly-out.
  9. Pulling the gaze, in this order: staging (people walk into your view), sound, a companion turning his head, light, and only then a slow nudge that gives way to the mouse. At most 3 nudges in the whole chapter, at least 60 s apart, each only after 12 s of not looking.
  10. One cinematic only: the prologue slip turns into the out-of-body fly-out. Everything else is played.

4.6 Comfort and screen

  • New settings: Head motion Full / Reduced / Off; Story camera Guided / Gentle (no nudges, and the slip becomes a plain eyelid close); Heartbeat zoom On / Off; a comfort dot; field of view 80-110.
  • Mouse is 1:1: no smoothing, no lag.
  • The screen no longer darkens when you look down at the snow: the vignette is lighter and body effects only change the image while they are active.
  • Render defaults [Director decides]: frame generation "Auto" = 2x (3x or 4x only when the real frame rate stays at 55 fps or more); motion blur off. The play-tests ran 4x frame generation on about 29-30 real frames per second (sessions 20261119_203955 and 20261119_222446), which is the most likely cause of the smearing.

5. Places and art direction

The art direction (the director's own document, 2026-11-03) is the yardstick: quiet awe, attention moving between the few metres at your feet and the far view, a sky that carries the light, crisp ridge silhouettes, blue snow in shade, warm light only on the high ridges, the climber small in a big world.

5.1 The expedition camp at Dingboche

[Built] today, rebuilt 2026-11-19 with Meshy models:

  • a gentle crescent of two-person dome tents, about 3.9 m apart, all doors to the trail; the last one is Sangye's
  • the mess dome and the kitchen tent side by side behind them
  • blue barrels and dried yak-dung fuel behind the kitchen, a pile of duffels at the mess door, porter loads ready at the row's end by the trail, the toilet tent at the back edge
  • a prayer pole in front of the camp, with flag lines to the mess tent and to two poles at the ends of the row

Still wrong (director, 2026-11-19): the camp is not pretty enough; the flags float and look fake; the mess-dome windows have jagged edges; the tents are scaled up (x1.5, and Sangye's x1.8) so the first-person body fits inside, which makes them larger than real expedition tents.

[Proposed] Camp redesign rules:

  1. Real sizes first. A two-person dome is about 2.2 × 1.4 m and 1.1 m tall. Solve the "body pokes through" problem in the camera (the seated posture and soft look limits) before scaling a tent.
  2. The camp is lived in: boots in vestibules, a pot steaming at the kitchen door, a cook singing, washing on a guy line, stones on the tent skirts against the wind, a path worn between tents.
  3. Composition from the tent door: when Sangye stands up, the frame holds the crescent, Dolma running in, the flags backlit, and a far white summit. The world builder checks it as it already checks other key frames.
  4. One warm colour accent per view (an orange tent, a red duffel) against blue shade.

5.2 Sangye's tent (the opening)

  • He wakes lying, eye 32 cm above the floor, head on a stuff sack. The door glows 30-40° to the left; the light comes through the fabric; the frosted inner wall is on the right; the duffel lies past his feet, lower right. The world builder checks these angles.
  • Standing up outside is the reveal of the camp.
  • [To build] a proper tent interior (inner tent, mats, sleeping bags). The Meshy domes have no inside yet.

5.3 The trail

The real trail from Dingboche up the valley to the Thukla pass, on our 1:1 terrain: river sounds, a frozen stream, a narrow stretch with a drop on one side where the porters pass, and the afternoon cloud that rises up the valley.

5.4 The Thukla memorials (the peak)

  • [Built] Meshy chortens in three kinds (a 2 m stepped chorten with a plaque, a 1.2 m cairn with a flag stick, a 3 m chorten with a pole), grouped like the real memorials: a dense field on the father's side of the trail, framing his stone, thinning out up the ridge, a few across the trail, flag lines between them.
  • The father's stone: a new stone, sharp fresh cuts, one smooth face; the name is a decal so it can change.
  • Framing: kneeling at the stone puts the stone in the lower-left third of the view and the plume 25-35° to the right and 12-18° up. The world builder computes the kneeling spot from that.
  • Respect rules [Proposed]: no real climbers' names on any plaque (unreadable or fictional only); the player can kneel only at his father's stone; nothing at the memorials can be climbed or knocked over.

5.5 Prayer flags, done realistically

The director asked (2026-11-19) for every flag line on the map to be redesigned. Today the flags are flat cards on a straight cord; they float and their sag is capped at 45 cm.

What real prayer flags are like [To verify with a Sherpa consultant]:

  • Horizontal lines (lungta, "wind horse") in five colours in a fixed, repeating order: blue, white, red, green, yellow. Our builder already uses this order.
  • Each flag is a printed cotton or synthetic rectangle with a wind-horse image and prayers, sewn along its top edge onto the cord, close together.
  • Lines run from high points: poles, roof corners, chortens, cairns, rocks. They sag in a long curve and never touch the ground.
  • Old lines are faded and shredded by sun and wind, often next to bright new ones.
  • Vertical poles with a long banner (darchor / tarchen) stand near houses and camps.

How we build them [Proposed]:

Clotha few Meshy or downloaded flag meshes with real thickness and a frayed edge

6.2 What the director said about them

  • Faces: realistic. Keep them.
  • Clothes: "extremely bad", "ultra bad" (2026-11-15 and again 2026-11-19): collars float off the neck with a dark gap; fabrics show no texture and read as smooth plastic.
  • Walking: feet slide and never leave the ground; no prints in the snow under the NPCs' feet.

The reason is known. The clothes were cut from the body surface in Blender by a script. A script cannot fake cloth simulation and texture craft. Hand-modelling realistic clothes in code has stopped (director, 2026-11-15).

6.4 Performance limits for the cast

The laptop has a 12 GB graphics card. At most 1-2 MetaHuman builds per editor session; clothing textures at 1k; NPC assets load in the background at chapter start and NPCs appear only behind closed eyelids, so a 2.7 s load freeze can never land on screen again.

The 17 measured checks before the director plays (camera spec §11) include: no view change the player did not cause beyond the speed limits; never walking at knee height (eye at least 120 cm in every frame above 0.6 m/s); every cut behind closed eyes; at most 3 nudges; no freeze over 100 ms with the eyes open; the rig costs at most 0.05 ms per frame; the look of the body and the key frames is judged by the director.
